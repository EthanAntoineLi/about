01

The short version

I'm in my final year at The British School of Guangzhou. Most of what I do outside lessons comes back to one idea: a robot that could reach someone who has fallen on a mountain, and do a job that would otherwise put a rescue team on dangerous ground. The arm is the first piece; the drone and the software that ties them together are next. None of it is finished — that is the point of the drawing set.

There is also Nora, a personal AI I built for myself and use every day, and a website that documents all of it — including the parts that don't work yet.

02

Why rescue

I ski, and every winter brings the same shape of accident: a person down on a line that is difficult, exposed, or both. That is the accident the EPQ brief is for — an arm that can work where a person would be exposed.

The long version of the project is one robot that does the approach and the rescue itself, with a drone working further out as its eye. I started with the arm because it is the part that has to touch a person — everything else can be built around it.

03

How I build

I design in Shapr3D, print on a Bambu Lab A1 mini, and wire the result to an STM32F407 over RS485. The kinematics are Python; the browser model is three.js. My EPQ is the arm's write-up: six axes drawn, five built, a 500 g payload target, and a research paper next to the hardware.

I would rather show a part that survives a test than a render that looks good, so nothing on this site is labelled better than it is.

04

Off the bench

Two HKU summers — AI application development with Python (June 2025), then advanced physics (July 2026). A battery-materials lab in December 2025: graphene lithium electrode materials, coin cells, charge–discharge and EIS. Before that, a drone built from scratch at home.

Away from the bench: tennis singles for the school team, a charity stall that raised over RMB 2,600, and a school Social Impact Award. French is my first language; I've been at school in Guangzhou since 2019 and speak Chinese and English fluently.
